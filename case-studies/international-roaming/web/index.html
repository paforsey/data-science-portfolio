Methodology, uncertainty, and exclusions

What "simulation interval" means

The shaded band on the revenue chart is a simulation interval: the 5th–95th percentile range of a Monte Carlo simulation of the next 12 months, not a statistical confidence interval. It reflects modeled dispersion in the simulation, not sampling error in an estimator.

Response and depth sensitivity

Two independent sliders, each 1.00× (as fitted) to its diagnostic-derived ceiling (× response, × depth), 3 pre-simulated points apiece. They move separately because they come from separate models and separate diagnostics: response sensitivity controls how strongly price shifts an account out of full coverage (the State Model); depth sensitivity controls how much revenue is retained when an account lands in partial coverage (the Magnitude Model), and never affects whether an account counts as a purchaser, only how much they're worth once they do. Neither slider is a confidence bound or a second estimate of the truth; both are sensitivity scenarios exploring how the conclusion would change if either assumption is understated. The recommendation itself is read from the as-fitted model, both sliders at 1.00× (their base position), not from either ceiling; the 1.15× price evaluated at both ceilings simultaneously is reported separately as a disclosed robustness check. Only available under the Base economy: Expansion and Contraction were only simulated at both ceilings, so selecting either forces both sliders there.

Economic scenarios and price coverage

The Base economy uses the full evaluated price grid (13 points from 0.90× to 1.20×), unconditioned on any macro shift. Expansion and Contraction were only simulated at three prices (1.00×, 1.15×, and 1.20×), always at both sensitivity sliders' ceiling, because that is the resolution the macro simulation was run at. The 1.15× point is a fixed stress-test price (the joint-sensitivity case's peak) — it is not tied to whichever price is currently recommended. Selecting Expansion or Contraction restricts the slider to those three prices rather than substituting the nearest tested one.

Roaming-pass purchasers vs. wireless subscribers

This tool's purchaser measure counts roaming-pass purchasers: accounts with at least one roaming trip receiving partial or full paid coverage in the forward year, not wireless-subscription cancellations. The study does not estimate wireless-account churn, and no figure on this page should be read as one.

How the purchaser chart and summary are simulated

Purchasers are counted inside the same Monte Carlo loop that produces revenue: each iteration, an account counts as a purchaser if at least one of its forward trips lands in a non-null coverage state that draw. Because it comes from the same draws, purchasers stay properly paired with revenue: the p5–p95 band on the purchaser chart is a genuine simulation interval, not a derived or approximated range.

Where a paired difference is available (Expansion and Contraction, at the fixed 1.15× stress-test price only, where both revenue and purchasers were differenced iteration-by-iteration against the 1.00× run), the summary above uses it and marks it (paired). Everywhere else, "change vs. 1.00×" is the difference of two marginal medians; common random numbers are used across every price at a given response/depth sensitivity setting, which limits (but doesn't eliminate) the Monte Carlo noise this introduces.

The purchaser rate denominator ( accounts) is the forward-panel population the simulation actually draws purchases from: the account base with a modeled forward trip, not the full customer book.

Why the segment table's purchaser figures are different

Segment-level purchasers are a separate, deterministic calculation: per account, P(at least one purchase) = 1 − ∏ P(null coverage) across that account's forward trips, summed within segment. This assumes conditional independence of an account's trip-purchase events: no account-level dependence is modeled. It uses the same trip-probability data and grain as the segment revenue curve (both sliders at ceiling, unconditioned macro), so, like segment revenue, it carries no simulation interval and doesn't change with the controls above, unlike the simulated chart and summary.

Segment figures

Segment revenue does not reconcile exactly with the portfolio total shown above: it is a probability-weighted expected value, while the portfolio figure is the median of a simulated, right-skewed distribution: the two differ by construction, currently by about at the current price. The same is expected of segment vs. portfolio purchasers, for the same reason. Segment figures also exclude the Dormant segment, disclosed separately below.
