Methodology, uncertainty, and exclusions

What "simulation interval" means

The shaded band on the revenue chart is a simulation interval — the 5th–95th percentile range of a Monte Carlo simulation of the next 12 months, not a statistical confidence interval. It reflects modeled dispersion in the simulation, not sampling error in an estimator.

Behavioral case

"As fitted" uses the response and depth models exactly as fitted to historical data. "Joint stronger-response sensitivity" scales both models up (× response, × depth) to test what happens if customers are more price-sensitive than fitted — it is a sensitivity scenario, not a confidence bound or a second estimate of the truth, and it is the case the recommended price was selected under.

Economic scenarios and price coverage

The Base economy uses the full evaluated price grid (13 points from 0.90× to 1.20×), unconditioned on any macro shift. Expansion and Contraction were only simulated at three prices — 1.00×, the recommended price, and 1.20× — always under the joint-sensitivity case, because that is the resolution the macro simulation was run at. Selecting Expansion or Contraction restricts the slider to those three prices rather than substituting the nearest tested one.

Roaming-pass purchasers vs. wireless subscribers

This tool's purchaser measure counts roaming-pass purchasers — accounts with at least one roaming trip receiving partial or full paid coverage in the forward year — not wireless-subscription cancellations. The study does not estimate wireless-account churn, and no figure on this page should be read as one.

Why the segment table has purchaser figures and the chart above doesn't

Segment-level purchasers are computed as a deterministic expected value: per account, P(at least one purchase) = 1 − ∏ P(null coverage) across that account's forward trips, summed within segment. This assumes conditional independence of an account's trip-purchase events — no account-level dependence is modeled. It uses the same trip-probability data and grain as the segment revenue curve (joint-sensitivity case, unconditioned macro), so it carries no simulation interval, same as segment revenue.

The chart and decision summary above need something stronger: a properly simulated purchaser count — with a p5–p95 interval, paired against revenue from the same simulation draws — at every price, economic scenario, and behavioral case. That requires accumulating purchaser counts inside the notebook's Monte Carlo simulation loop alongside revenue, not a second independent simulation (which would break the pairing needed for a valid interval or a revenue-per-purchaser-lost figure). That work is not done yet — see web/README.md for the field-level data contract this page expects once it is.

Segment figures

Segment revenue does not reconcile exactly with the portfolio total shown above: it is a probability-weighted expected value, while the portfolio figure is the median of a simulated, right-skewed distribution — the two differ by construction, currently by about at the current price. Segment figures also exclude the Dormant segment, disclosed separately below, and are not simulated, so no interval is shown for them.
